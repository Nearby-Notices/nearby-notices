About

Public notices that affect where people live are published in The Gazette in legal language that is hard to read and hard to search by place. This tool finds the notices near a postcode or town and reads them back in plain English, each linked to the notice as published.

How the plain English is made, and checked

A language model reads the notice and returns the facts (what is changing, where, from when, who published it, how to object) and a two- or three-sentence summary. Every place name must appear word for word in the notice or it is dropped. Every summary sentence is then checked against the notice by a second step that must return a supporting quote that really is in the text; a sentence that fails is removed and counted. The model never writes advice: where a notice says how to object, that sentence is quoted, not composed.
